# Compound Technology Partners — Reference Video + Page Description

## Purpose

This file is the **single visual reference brief** for recreating the supplied Compound Technology Partners landing page.

Use the supplied reference video as the primary source of truth. Screenshots and the existing finalized design-system document support the video, but the video determines:

- visual state
- scroll sequence
- section relationships
- transitions
- image reveals
- motion
- changes in density
- exact compositional behavior

This is a recreation, not a redesign.

Do not introduce a new visual direction.

---

# 1. Overall Impression

The page is a black editorial technology site built from a rigid architectural grid.

The design combines three opposing qualities:

**luxury editorial typography + technical interface structure + degraded photographic imagery.**

The result should feel:

- premium
- intelligent
- cinematic
- architectural
- precise
- experimental
- enterprise credible

It should not feel like:

- generic SaaS
- generic AI startup
- Web3
- cyberpunk
- neon futurism
- normal consulting
- a portfolio full of effects

The dominant visual material is black.

White/off-white typography and thin rules create structure.

Photography introduces the only meaningful color.

---

# 2. Video-Level Behavior

The reference video is not just a sequence of static screens.

It repeatedly reveals the page through:

- black pauses
- small centered Compound branding
- slow content appearance
- degraded imagery
- subtle section transitions
- controlled scrolling
- occasional transition-like black frames

The page should feel as though it is **revealing itself**, rather than continuously animating.

Motion is subdued.

Nothing should bounce.

Nothing should feel like a typical startup landing-page animation.

---

# 3. Global Page Frame

The website lives inside a very dark black canvas.

There is a subtle technical frame around the viewport/content area.

The header sits inside this system and contains:

### Left

Small timestamp / technical indicator.

### Center

Compound mark.

### Right

Navigation:

`HOME`

`ABOUT`

`SERVICES`

A thin horizontal rule anchors the header.

The header typography is tiny and restrained.

It should never compete with the hero.

---

# 4. Hero — First Major Screen

The hero is the dominant visual moment.

## Headline

Large serif headline:

**Frontier AI for your business**

`your business` is visually distinct and italicized.

The headline spans most of the available width.

The serif is extremely large, thin/elegant, and tightly set.

The hero typography does most of the visual work.

Do not make the hero bold.

Do not add text shadows.

Do not use gradients.

---

# 5. Hero Information Strip

Immediately underneath the headline is a continuous technical strip.

It is divided into several structured zones rather than separate floating cards.

Visible information includes:

`CUSTOM AI AGENTS FOR THE MID MARKET.`

`AI TRANSFORMATION FOR CRITICAL INDUSTRIES.`

`CONTACT US →`

There is also a technical globe/orbit-like icon.

The strip has:

- thin borders
- tiny typography
- dark technical texture
- compact vertical proportions
- square corners

The strip is part of the architectural grid.

---

# 6. Hero Photography

The hero image is large and horizontal.

Its visual language includes:

- American flag
- astronaut
- dark landscape / ground
- dense low-resolution photographic texture

The image is intentionally degraded.

It should look processed rather than like a normal photograph.

Visible treatment includes:

- halftone / dot structure
- pixel clusters
- severe tonal reduction
- grain
- black negative space
- red/blue/cyan image-derived color
- slight channel irregularity

The astronaut is visually bright relative to the rest of the image.

The image remains darker than the typography.

There are small technical markers/crosshairs integrated into the image/grid.

---

# 7. Hero Reveal

At the beginning of the video the hero can appear through an almost-black state before settling into the complete composition.

The image should not simply fade from a clean image.

Its reveal should feel like:

**degraded / obscured → recognizable → stable degraded image**

The final state retains the processing.

---

# 8. Black Transition Frames

The video contains moments where the screen becomes almost entirely black.

A small Compound mark appears near the center.

This should be understood as a transition language.

It is not a traditional loading spinner.

Use it only for intentional transitions.

The visual sequence is approximately:

**black field → tiny centered Compound mark → next page state**

This transition should be slow and restrained.

---

# 9. Scrolling / Transition Philosophy

Scrolling should expose sections progressively.

Avoid aggressive scroll-jacking.

Avoid:

- excessive parallax
- large scale changes
- dramatic 3D
- velocity-based gimmicks
- persistent animated backgrounds

The page can use subtle reveal timing as content enters view, but the user should still feel in control of the scroll.

# 10. What We Do / Services

The services area appears as a large photographic/technical field.

The three service concepts are:

### 01 — Discover

We embed with your team to understand your business. Interviews, workflow mapping, and rigorous documentation to help find where AI actually moves the needle.

### 02 — Build

We turn strategy into working software with custom agents built on top of your tools and embedded AI in your products. Built for your workflows, not generic use cases.

### 03 — Adopt

We don’t just hand over a working system, we make sure it gets adopted. We redesign workflows around it, train your people, and stay until it sticks.

The three blocks are deliberately staggered.

The section should feel more like a technical image/diagram than a standard three-column SaaS card section.

# 16. Editorial Quote Sequence

The video also contains a separate editorial moment with:

**“The measure of intelligence is the ability to change.”**

Author:

`- ALBERT EINSTEIN`

This is dramatically different from the client testimonial.

Use a large editorial serif.

The quote is paired with heavily processed historical artwork resembling **Washington Crossing the Delaware**.

The image uses the same degraded material language as the hero:

- halftone
- dark tonal treatment
- grain
- image-derived color
- low-resolution texture

The quote should feel like an editorial interlude rather than another marketing card.

---

# 17. Grid Language

The entire page is based on a persistent architectural grid.

The grid should feel like:

- blueprint
- Swiss editorial layout
- technical drawing
- engineering system

Use:

- 12-column desktop grid
- 1px rules
- subtle opacity
- consistent vertical guides
- repeated alignment anchors

Asymmetry must always happen **inside the grid**.

Never use random absolute coordinates to imitate the screenshot.

---

# 18. Spacing

The page deliberately contains large amounts of black space.

Use a 4px base system.

Core rhythm:

`4 / 8 / 12 / 16 / 24 / 32 / 48 / 64 / 80 / 96 / 128 / 160 / 192 / 224 / 256 / 280`

Large editorial sections can have very generous vertical spacing.

Do not compact the page simply to fit more content.

---

# 19. Shape Language

Everything is geometric.

Use:

- rectangles
- squares
- straight rules
- brackets
- crosshairs
- grids
- occasional technical circles

Do not use:

- pills
- rounded rectangles
- bubbles
- floating soft cards
- excessive shadows

Default:

`border-radius: 0`

---

# 20. Typography Behavior

Primary editorial face:

**Instrument Serif**

Neutral UI/content face:

**Geist Sans**

Technical metadata:

**Geist Mono**

The page relies on typography scale rather than font-weight extremes.

The hero is extremely large.

The serif quote is extremely large.

Body is quiet.

Labels are tiny.

Metadata is tracked.

Secondary text is clearly lower contrast than primary text.

---

# 21. Image Processing Rules

Photography is treated as physical/digital material.

Images should frequently exhibit:

- halftone dots
- thresholded areas
- pixel clusters
- downsampling
- slight channel offset
- grain
- dark overlays

Use image-derived color only.

Do not introduce bright interface colors.

Photography should remain subordinate to the typography.

---

# 22. Texture Rules

There are extremely subtle dark technical textures.

The most useful texture is:

**dark-on-dark diagonal micro-lines**

The texture should only become obvious when inspected closely.

There is also very subtle global grain.

Suggested grain opacity:

`0.02–0.05`

Do not make the page visibly noisy everywhere.

---

# 23. Motion Rules

Motion is almost subliminal.

Micro interactions:

`200–400ms`

Text reveals:

`600–1200ms`

Section reveals:

`800–1500ms`

Ambient image movement:

`8–20s`

Intentional navigation transition:

`500–1000ms`

Preferred easing:

`cubic-bezier(0.16, 1, 0.3, 1)`

Text entrance:

- opacity 0 → 1
- translateY 12–30px → 0

No bounce.

No spring.

No exaggerated scale.

---

# 24. Ambient Image Movement

Images may move extremely slightly.

Maximum visible displacement:

**approximately 1–4px**

Possible animation dimensions:

- x
- y
- tiny scale
- evolving noise

The movement should be difficult to notice.

The page should feel alive, not animated.

---

# 25. Hover Behavior

Buttons:

- slight background shift
- subtle border brightening
- arrow moves approximately 5–10px

Cards:

- tiny image brightness/clarity adjustment
- icon moves 1–2px

Do not enlarge cards dramatically.

---

# 26. Responsive Behavior

Desktop preserves the full architectural grid.

Tablet reduces the number of columns while preserving the technical rules.

Mobile stacks content but keeps:

- black space
- editorial typography
- technical labels
- 1px rules
- asymmetric offsets where practical

Never turn the mobile version into generic centered cards.

---

# 27. What Matters Most

When visually comparing the implementation with the reference, prioritize:

## 1. Hero geometry

The first screen must immediately look correct.

## 2. Typography

Especially:

- serif choice
- hero size
- hero line height
- italic phrase
- tracking
- wrapping

## 3. Grid

Vertical rules and alignment must feel intentional.

## 4. Image treatment

The images must visibly be degraded.

Clean stock photography is wrong.

## 5. Negative space

Do not squeeze the page.

## 6. Asymmetry

Cards and plates should feel deliberately offset.

## 7. Restraint

If unsure, use less animation, less color, less shadow, and less decoration.

---

# 28. Implementation Rule

The reference should determine the visual result.

The implementation should not reinterpret the reference.

When something is ambiguous:

1. inspect the supplied video
2. inspect the relevant screenshot
3. use the established design-system rules
4. choose the option that introduces the least visual invention

Do not make a design decision simply because it is a common web-development pattern.

---

# 29. Definition of Done

The recreation is complete only when:

- the first viewport has the same visual hierarchy
- the hero typography occupies approximately the same visual mass
- the grid aligns across sections
- the image treatments feel materially similar
- section spacing feels equally sparse
- asymmetry is preserved
- the CTA remains restrained
- the footer remains tiny
- motion does not call attention to itself
- mobile preserves the same identity

The final test is:

**Does it look like the reference website, or merely like a dark website inspired by it?**

Only the first outcome is acceptable.
