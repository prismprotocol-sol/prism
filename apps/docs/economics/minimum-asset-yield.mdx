---
title: "Why the asset has to earn 15% or more"
description: "The senior floor is set outside the pool, and senior is the largest layer."
---

pPRIME cannot pay less than about 7%, because that floor is set outside the pool
by what investors can earn elsewhere. Senior is the largest layer, so it sets
most of the cost of funds.

Work backwards and a pool needs roughly **15–17% gross** on the underlying loans to
pay the senior layer, the junior layers, the losses, the fees and still leave the
originator a return worth having.

## The chain of constraints

<Steps>
  <Step title="Maple pays ~5% at near-zero risk">
    On overcollateralised, zero-loss, instantly redeemable paper.
  </Step>
  <Step title="So pPRIME has a floor around 7%">
    An unrated receivables pool must pay meaningfully more, or there is no reason to
    hold it.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Senior is the largest layer">
    So that floor drives most of the blended cost of funds.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add the junior layers, losses and fees">
    pCORE at ~11%, pALPHA at ~16%, plus assumed losses and PRISM's fees.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The book must earn 15–17% gross">
    Anything less does not leave the originator a return worth having.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## What this rules out

<Warning>
  This rules out safe, low-yield assets. Invoice finance against large rated
  corporates earns about 10% and cannot support the structure, even with a layer
  removed and every rate cut.
</Warning>

That is a real constraint on what PRISM can fund, not a preference. It means PRISM
is structurally limited to higher-yielding books — invoice financiers, BNPL
lenders, merchant financiers — and the credit risk that comes with them.

It is also why [a loan book earning at least 15% gross](/originators/requirements)
is a hard requirement for originators.
