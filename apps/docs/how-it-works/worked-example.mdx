## How the pool is funded

A pool holds \$10M of receivables.

The originator deposits \$1.5M of its own money — 15%. A bank advances \$7.5M. The
remaining \$1M is what PRISM raises from investors.

| Source | Amount | Share of pool |
|---|---|---|
| Bank senior advance | \$7.5M | 75% |
| Raised by PRISM from investors | \$1M | 10% |
| Originator's own money, deposited first | \$1.5M | 15% |
| **Pool of receivables** | **\$10M** | **100%** |

## Who absorbs a loss

If the pool loses \$400,000, the originator absorbs all of it. Investors lose
nothing. The originator would have to lose its entire \$1.5M before pALPHA is
touched at all.

The table below reads off the [attachment points](/concepts/attachment-points).
It shows the order in which capital is destroyed, not a forecast of what will
happen.

| Pool loss | Who absorbs it |
|---|---|
| \$400,000 · 4% | The originator, entirely. No investor is touched. |
| \$1.5M · 15% | The originator's deposit is exactly exhausted. Still no investor loss. |
| Above 15% | pALPHA · junior begins to lose. |
| Above 21–25% | pCORE · mezzanine begins to lose. |
| Higher still | The senior layer begins to lose. |

<Note>
  The 4% figure used above is PRISM's expected loss rate. It has never been
  measured against real data and is a placeholder. See [Disclosures](/disclosures).
</Note>

## What this example does not show

It does not show the originator diverting collections, misreporting the loan tape,
or failing outright. Those are not absorbed by the waterfall at all — they are
addressed by the [four controls](/risk/controls), and they are the reason the
[originator is the concentrated risk](/concepts/the-originator-problem).
