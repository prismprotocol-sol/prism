## What you are buying

A note against a pool of short-dated receivables, in one of three layers. Each
layer sits at a different point in the loss waterfall and is priced accordingly.

| Note | Target rate | Starts losing when |
|---|---|---|
| pPRIME · senior | 7.5–9% | Pool losses exceed 40% |
| pCORE · mezzanine | ~11% | Pool losses reach 21–25% |
| pALPHA · junior | ~16% | Pool losses reach 15–21% |

Beneath all three sits the originator's own money, at least 15% of the pool,
which is destroyed first.

<Note>
  Rates are targets, not guarantees. They are our estimates of what each layer
  should pay, and they will move when real buyers price them. See
  [Disclosures](/disclosures).
</Note>

## Choosing a layer

<Columns cols={3}>
  <Card title="pPRIME · senior" icon="shield">
    Paid first, loses last. The largest and cheapest layer. Often already filled by
    the originator's own bank, in which case PRISM does not raise it.
  </Card>
  <Card title="pCORE · mezzanine" icon="layers">
    The middle. For professional investors who want more than senior pays without
    taking the junior position.
  </Card>
  <Card title="pALPHA · junior" icon="chart-candlestick">
    Paid last, loses early. Intended for a professional credit investor who also
    judges the originator.
  </Card>
</Columns>

## Who holds pALPHA matters

The junior note is not just the highest-paying layer. It is intended for an
investor who does their own diligence on the originator — and whose willingness to
hold it at a given price is itself the signal about whether the pool should exist.

That is also why PRISM's 4% expected loss rate is a placeholder: the price a real
credit investor demands for pALPHA is one of the two things that will replace it.

## The pool term

Pools run 30 to 90 days. There is no redemption before maturity in version one —
see [liquidity](/investors/liquidity).
